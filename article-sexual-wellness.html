SEXUAL WELLNESS

Sexual Wellness & Healthy Relationships

Sexual wellness is about more than sexual activity. It includes communication, consent, comfort, personal boundaries, privacy, safety and respect between people.

1. Understand Sexual Wellness

Sexual wellness involves having accurate information, making informed decisions and being able to communicate about personal boundaries and relationship expectations.

It can also include emotional wellbeing, mutual respect and knowing when to seek appropriate health information or professional support.

Remember:
Healthy intimacy should involve mutual respect, clear communication, freely given consent and consideration for each person's comfort and boundaries.

2. Communication Matters

Clear communication can help people understand each other's expectations, comfort levels and boundaries.

  • Talk openly and respectfully.
  • Listen to your partner.
  • Ask questions when something is unclear.
  • Do not assume that silence means agreement.
  • Respect a person's answer.

3. Consent Should Be Clear

Consent means freely agreeing to something. It should not be obtained through pressure, threats, manipulation or fear.

Consent can change. A person can change their mind at any point, and that decision should be respected.

  • Consent should be voluntary.
  • Consent should be informed.
  • Consent should be specific to the situation.
  • Consent can be withdrawn.
  • Each person is responsible for respecting boundaries.

4. Respect Personal Boundaries

Everyone has different comfort levels. A healthy relationship makes space for people to communicate what they are comfortable with and what they are not.

A boundary is not something that needs to be debated or mocked. It should be communicated and respected.

5. Safer Sexual Health Decisions

People can make informed decisions about sexual health by learning about appropriate protection, sexually transmitted infections, testing and reproductive health.

Reliable health information can help people understand available options and make decisions appropriate to their circumstances.

  • Learn about appropriate protection.
  • Understand STI prevention and testing.
  • Seek qualified healthcare advice when needed.
  • Discuss relevant health information with a partner.

6. Emotional Wellbeing Matters

Sexual wellbeing can also be connected with emotions, expectations, self-esteem and relationship satisfaction.

People may experience different emotions before or after intimate situations. Honest communication can help partners understand each other better.

7. Respect Privacy

Private conversations, photographs and personal information should be handled carefully.

  • Do not share private images without permission.
  • Do not access private accounts without permission.
  • Protect sensitive personal information.
  • Discuss digital boundaries openly.

8. Avoid Pressure and Coercion

No one should feel pressured into intimate activity because of fear, guilt, threats, financial dependence or repeated unwanted requests.

Respect means accepting another person's decision, even when that decision is different from what you hoped for.

9. Know When to Seek Professional Help

Questions about sexual health can sometimes require professional guidance. A qualified healthcare professional can provide information about symptoms, testing, contraception, reproductive health and other health concerns.

If an intimate relationship involves fear, coercion, violence or abuse, seeking appropriate support can also be important.

10. Build Healthy Intimacy

Healthy intimacy can develop through trust, communication, respect and emotional connection.

There is no single definition of a healthy relationship for everyone. What matters is that people communicate honestly, respect boundaries, make informed decisions and treat each other with dignity.

Key Takeaways

  • Sexual wellness includes physical, emotional and relationship wellbeing.
  • Clear communication helps people understand boundaries and expectations.
  • Consent should be freely given, informed and respected.
  • Consent can be withdrawn at any time.
  • Personal and digital privacy should be respected.
  • Learning about sexual health can support informed decisions.
  • Pressure, threats and coercion are not healthy forms of communication.
  • Professional healthcare advice can be useful for specific health concerns.
